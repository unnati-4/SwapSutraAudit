01 — The read

Your problem isn't reach. It's that you're posting the wrong category.

Before a single new reel, this is the diagnosis the rest of the manual is built on.

India already has thousands of accounts posting book reviews, book stacks and aesthetic shelf photos. If SwapSutra posts those, it competes with people who do it full-time for fun — and loses.

Low traction on a book account almost never means the videos are badly made. It means the account is interchangeable with fifty others. The algorithm has nothing to distinguish, so it distributes nothing.

SwapSutra has one thing no bookstagrammer in India has: real books physically moving between real strangers, and the data trail of those movements. That is a category of content nobody else is allowed to make. It is also, conveniently, entirely faceless.

So the strategic bet is simple: stop being a book account and become a book-movement account. Reviews are commodity. Circulation is proprietary. Every pillar, script and video in this manual comes from that one shift.

The second thing worth naming honestly: at 3–5 hours a week you cannot win on volume. You win on ownability and repetition — a small number of recurring, recognisable formats that people start to expect. Three named series beat thirty one-off posts.

03 — Pillars

Five pillars, and what each one is for

Each pillar has a job in the funnel. Don't judge them all by the same metric — the confessions pillar exists to farm comments, not signups.

The Travelling Book30%

Follow one real copy as it moves between readers and cities. Each episode adds a reader, a city, a line they wrote before passing it on. Runs as a numbered series with a fixed opening shot.

JobBrand memory + shareability. This is the format people describe to their friends.

Found In Books20%

Inscriptions, train tickets, pressed flowers, margin notes, grocery lists — the objects and handwriting found in secondhand and swapped books. Sourced from your own community by open call.

JobSaves and shares. Emotional, screenshot-able, costs you nothing to produce.

Reading Economics20%

The money side of reading in India. New vs secondhand vs swap pricing, what a shelf is worth, how to read thirty books for nothing, Sunday market versus online. Numbers on screen.

JobSearch + intent. This is what converts, because it makes the product the obvious answer.

Swap Mechanics15%

Screen recordings of the real product: listing a book in eighteen seconds, the near-you feed, handover proof, what happens if someone doesn't return. Clean, fast, no music swells.

JobObjection handling. Trust is the actual barrier, not interest.

Reader Confessions15%

Opinions and shared guilt: unread shelves, DNF permission, TBR piles, reading slumps, the plastic-cover habit. Text on screen, one strong line, deliberately arguable.

JobComments and reach. Cheap to make, feeds the algorithm, warms the audience for the rest.

Weekly mix7 posts

5 short videos + 2 carousels per week, drawn in this ratio. Over a month that's roughly 6 Travelling Book, 4 Found In Books, 4 Reading Economics, 3 Swap Mechanics, 3 Confessions, and 8 carousels split across pillars.

RuleNever two posts from the same pillar back to back.

04 — Operating system

The four-hour week

One batch session and four short daily windows. This is a genuine sequence, so it's numbered — each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Sun
    Block A
    Shoot once, harvest all week

    One table, one window, one stack of books. Capture 20–25 clips of generic B-roll: page flips, spines sliding out, a book being wrapped, hands stacking, a shelf pan, a book on a table with tea. This footage gets reused for months across every pillar — you are building a library, not shooting a video.

    45 min
  2. Sun
    Block B
    Write five hooks, then five scripts

    Pull five hooks from the bank in section 07. Write the hook first and the script second — if the hook doesn't survive being read aloud, kill it before you spend editing time on it. Keep each script under 110 words for a 45-second cut.

    35 min
  3. Sun
    Block C
    Cut, caption, schedule

    Assemble in CapCut from the B-roll library, auto-caption, correct the caption timing, export 1080×1920. Schedule all five plus two carousels through Meta Business Suite. Leave one slot open for something reactive.

    60 min
  4. Mon–Fri
    Daily
    The engagement window

    Same time each day. Reply to every comment on your last post — the first 60 minutes after publishing matter most. Then leave ten substantive comments on Indian book accounts and in city-specific posts. Not "great post": a related experience, a real disagreement, a question. This is how a small account gets discovered.

    15 min / day
  5. Wed
    Midweek
    Work the inbox

    Answer every DM. Personally message anyone who signed up but hasn't listed a book yet — one line, no template energy. This single habit moves more first swaps than any post.

    20 min
  6. Sat
    Review
    Read the numbers, set next week

    Top three and bottom three posts, by shares and saves rather than likes. Write down the one thing the top three had in common. Choose next week's five hooks from that pattern. Log signups and completed swaps against the previous week.

    25 min

Total: about 4 hours. If a week collapses, protect the daily engagement window over the batch session. An account that posts three times and engages daily outgrows one that posts seven times and disappears.

How each long-form video pays for itself

Shoot the long-form first, then harvest. From one 10-minute video you should get three or four Shorts, one Instagram carousel, one WhatsApp broadcast and a week of story frames. That's the only way weekly long-form fits inside four hours — it doesn't sit on top of the short-form workload, it replaces the writing half of it.

Search-first titling

YouTube is a search engine in a way Instagram isn't. Title for what someone types: "How to read 30 books a year in India (without buying them)" outperforms "My reading journey" every time. Put the same phrase in the first line of the description and say it aloud in the first fifteen seconds.

Ad revenue will not be the profit. It's the smallest line on the list.

Reported Indian CPMs for general-interest content cluster in a wide, low range, and book content sits at the modest end of it. Treat published RPM figures as directional only, and expect a channel doing 100,000 views a month to produce pocket money rather than income. That is not a reason to skip YouTube — it's a reason to be clear about what YouTube is for.

YouTube's real job here is to be the top of the funnel that Instagram can't be: searchable, evergreen, and long enough to build actual trust before someone hands a book to a stranger.

10 — Conversion

From a view to a completed swap

Most book accounts lose people between the video and the app. Six steps, each with one job.

See
The video does one job: make them curious enough to tap the profile

Not to convert. Conversion happens on the profile, never in the caption. Stop putting links in captions — they suppress reach and nobody taps them anyway.

Profile
Bio names the outcome and the city, not the category

"Swap books with readers near you · Bengaluru, Pune, Delhi NCR · first month free" beats "India's book community". Pin three posts: the best Travelling Book episode, the 40-second swap demo, and the ₹0 reading guide.

Link
The link goes to a city page, never the homepage

Three options only: Books near you in [city] · How swapping works, 60 seconds · Free: the ₹0 Reading Guide. Every extra link costs you conversions.

Capture
Comment-to-DM on every high-reach post

"Comment SWAP" triggers an automatic DM with the link. It converts far better than bio traffic, and the comments themselves boost distribution. Follow the auto-DM with a real one within a day.

Activate
Signup is not the win. The first listing is.

A user with zero books listed will never swap. Message every new signup within 48 hours with one specific ask: "List three books you're finished with — takes about a minute each."

Swap
Hand-hold the first swap personally, for as long as you can

Until you're at real volume, matching people yourself is the highest-leverage hour in your week. A completed first swap is the strongest predictor of a paying member — and it produces the next Travelling Book episode.

Three lead magnets worth making

MagnetFormatWhy this one
The ₹0 Reading Guide6-page PDFDirectly downstream of your best-performing pillar. Ends with SwapSutra as method five, not method one.
The Book PassportPrintable insertA slip readers print, sign and leave inside a swapped book. It's a lead magnet, a physical branding object, and an endless content source in one — every returned passport is a Travelling Book episode.
Shelf AuditSheet or simple web toolCount your unread books, see what they cost, see what they'd swap for. Turns a vague feeling into a number, and the number is the motivation.

WhatsApp is the channel that actually converts in India. Instagram builds the audience; WhatsApp closes it. Run one group per active city, capped and curated, plus a weekly broadcast of new listings near them. Design one story-and-status-shaped asset per week so members can forward it without editing — forwarding is the only distribution that costs you nothing.

11 — Density

Three cities, not one country

A swap network is worthless at 1% density in twenty cities and valuable at 20% density in one neighbourhood. This is the least intuitive and most important part of the plan.

Pull your Users sheet and rank cities by signups. Pick the top two, plus one campus city where the Ambassador system gives you a shortcut. Everything geo-targeted goes to those three for the full 90 days.

13 — Schedule

The 90 days

Three phases, in order. Don't start phase two until phase one's target is hit — the sequence is the point.

Phase 01

Build the machine

Days 1–30
  • Rewrite bio and link page; pin three posts
  • Shoot the B-roll library — one long session, 60+ clips
  • Ship scripts S01–S15; establish the Sunday ritual
  • Launch The Travelling Book as a named, numbered series
  • Open the call for Found In Books submissions
  • Pick the three cities from your Users sheet
  • Publish the ₹0 Reading Guide; turn on comment-to-DM
TargetConsistent 7 posts/week and a first-listing rate above 40%
Phase 02

Find what works

Days 31–60
  • Launch YouTube: one long-form per week, harvested into Shorts
  • Publish "What India Actually Reads" from your own data
  • Double down on the two pillars with the best share rate
  • First city meetup; film it faceless
  • Recruit campus ambassadors in city three
  • Open WhatsApp groups for the top two cities
  • Ship the Book Passport
TargetOne post per week clearing 10× your normal reach, and a repeatable reason why
Phase 03

Compound it

Days 61–90
  • Cut low-performing pillars; reallocate their share
  • Re-cut the top five posts as city-specific versions
  • Collaborate with 3–5 Indian book creators, swap-led not promo-led
  • Push toward the 500-subscriber fan-funding gate
  • Convert the first free-trial cohort to ₹49 members
  • Approach two publishers or bookstores for sponsorship
  • Write the house-style document from your thirteen Saturday sentences
TargetSwaps growing week on week without you matching people by hand
14 — Guardrails

Things that will cost you the next 90 days

Each of these is a specific way small book accounts stall.

  • Generic book reviews and "5 books you must read" lists. Infinite supply, zero differentiation, and they attract followers who will never swap anything.
  • Launching in ten cities at once. A reader who opens the app and finds nobody nearby does not come back, and you only get one first impression per city.
  • Buying followers or engagement. It poisons your reach distribution permanently and tells you nothing true about what's working.
  • Posting the same file to Instagram, YouTube and everywhere else. Re-cut per platform — different length, different first frame, different caption. A visible watermark from another app suppresses reach.
  • Leading with the ₹49 membership. Sell the first swap. Sell the membership to people who've already completed one and felt it work.
  • Inventing statistics because the real ones are small. "Seven readers" told honestly is more compelling than "ten thousand books" that isn't true, and it's the only version you can defend.
  • Chasing trending audio that has nothing to do with books. It buys a spike of the wrong audience and teaches the algorithm to show you to people who won't convert.
  • Abandoning a series after four episodes. Serialised formats pay off exactly when they start to feel repetitive to you — that's the moment they've become recognisable to everyone else.
  • Skipping the daily engagement window because you posted. Posting is broadcast; commenting is distribution. At your size the second matters more.

Both are directional and change frequently — verify against YouTube's own documentation before making decisions that depend on them.
